# Facts ledger, Loophole 001

Every factual claim in the narration or on screen: the wording we used, how it is hedged, and at least one reputable source.
The grandmother story is fiction and is presented as a story ("Tonight, the time machine works").

| # | Claim (wording used) | Hedge / notes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In the 1980s, physicist Igor Novikov had an idea." History is like a filmed movie: you can be in it, you can't edit it. | Called "an idea", never a proven law. Novikov developed the self consistency principle in the mid 1980s (he had raised the idea earlier, in books from 1975 and 1983). The film analogy is our simplification of "only self consistent histories are allowed". | Novikov self consistency principle, Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novikov_self-consistency_principle |
| 2 | "Scientists tested it with pool balls. On paper." A ball goes into a time tunnel, comes out earlier and tries to knock its younger self away. "The math says no." | "On paper" = a theoretical analysis, not an experiment. The puzzle was raised by Joseph Polchinski; the analysis by Echeverria, Klinkhammer and Thorne. "No solution" refers to the paradox trajectory: no self consistent solution is one where the young ball is knocked away and never enters. | F. Echeverria, G. Klinkhammer, K. S. Thorne, "Billiard balls in wormhole spacetimes with closed timelike curves: Classical theory", Phys. Rev. D 44, 1077 (1991): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.44.1077 |
| 3 | "The only shot that works knocks the young ball in." | Simplified. The paper found self consistent "glancing blow" solutions: the older ball deflects its younger self only slightly, sending it into the wormhole at just the angle needed to deliver that same blow. It also found that some starting conditions allow more than one consistent solution, so "the only shot" is narrative shorthand for "the shots that work are the ones that send it in". | Same paper as row 2; summary in Novikov principle article above. |
| 4 | "You still choose. Inside a story that already has your name in it." | Interpretation, not a physics claim. Novikov argued free will is not restricted more than ordinary physics already restricts it. | Novikov self consistency principle, Wikipedia (section on free will): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novikov_self-consistency_principle |
| 5 | "One cosmonaut came back from space a fiftieth of a second in the future." On screen: "803 DAYS IN ORBIT", "+1/50 s INTO THE FUTURE", name tag SERGEI KRIKALEV. | Sources give about 0.02 s (Guinness says about 1/48 s) for Krikalev's roughly 803 days in orbit. "About a fiftieth of a second" is within that range. | Guinness World Records, Greatest time dilation: https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/79267-greatest-time-dilation ; Universe Today: https://www.universetoday.com/105650/cosmonaut-sergei-krikalev-the-worlds-most-prolific-time-traveler |

Claims that were cut or softened during research:
- The "your map drifts by about 10 km a day" GPS line was cut (contested derivation).
- "Eight hundred and three days" was moved from narration to an on screen readout to keep the line short; the figure (803 days, 9 hours, 39 minutes) is from Guinness.
- "Probability exactly zero" phrasing was not used; we say "the math says no" only about the paradox trajectory in the billiard analysis.
